# Philosophy

Memento Mori is not a productivity cult in a tasteful coat. It is a small reminder that time is the one budget nobody gets to refinance.

The point is deliberately simple: put mortality next to the system clock, where the user already looks, and make the abstraction of "someday" slightly harder to hide behind. A normal clock says what time it is. This widget asks whether the thing you are doing deserves that time. Annoying, yes. Useful, unfortunately also yes.

The classical idea is not depressive. In Stoic practice, Christian art, vanitas painting, and ordinary graveyard common sense, remembering death is a way to sharpen attention. Skulls, candles, hourglasses, bones, wilted flowers, and fading light are not gore props. They are compression artifacts for a fact the calendar keeps politely obscuring.

The tone should stay dry, not cruel. The app can be acidic because the subject is absurd: a person with finite years can still lose an evening arguing with a settings panel. But it should never shame users about illness, grief, age, disability, or medical risk. The joke is aimed at procrastination and self-deception, not at mortality itself.

Design rules:

- Keep the tray widget quiet. The status bar is expensive real estate.
- Let the full panel carry the art. The tray should whisper; the panel can raise an eyebrow.
- Use classical motifs without costume drama: bone, ash, onyx, candlelight, hourglass geometry.
- Prefer exact utility over mystical wellness copy.
- Keep all estimates visibly approximate. The model is a mirror, not an oracle.

Typography direction:

- Fraunces for the display clock and brand moments: old-style, elegant, slightly strange, and available under the SIL Open Font License.
- Geist for interface text: modern, functional, readable, and also available under the SIL Open Font License.
- System fallbacks remain mandatory. A mortality widget should not fail because a font CDN had a mood.

Commercial posture:

The source stays open because the premise is intimate enough that users should be able to inspect it. Paid installers are convenience, not a hostage situation. If someone wants to fork it and make the skull smile, that is between them and whatever remains of their taste.
